Ponnarmadha is a PhD student in the MEEG lab developing sustainable methods to produce bacterial cellulose (BC) — a versatile biomaterial with promising applications in agriculture, packaging, and climate-resilient material design. Her work tackles a central bottleneck in BC production: the cost of conventional growth media. By using agricultural and agro-industrial waste streams, particularly date palm hydrolysate, she is turning regional waste into a valuable biomaterial in line with circular bioeconomy principles.

Her project combines bacterial isolation and characterization with bioprocess optimization. To date, she has identified nine confirmed BC-producing strains from fruit and vegetable sources, refined fermentation parameters to maximize yield, and designed a novel bioreactor with increased air–interfacial area using date-fiber and stainless-steel mesh scaffolds. She is now translating the bacterial cellulose into temperature-responsive hydrogel containers for plant growth, a promising tool for water-efficient agriculture in arid climates.

The work sits at the intersection of microbial biotechnology, sustainable materials science, and arid agriculture, with direct relevance to UAE's challenges around date palm waste valorization, agricultural water efficiency, and climate resilience.
