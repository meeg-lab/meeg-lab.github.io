Elfa is a Master's student in Molecular Biology and Biotechnology at UAEU, working in the MEEG lab on the rhizosphere microbiomes of native UAE desert plants. Her thesis investigates whether root-associated bacterial communities are driven more strongly by host plant identity or by seasonal extremes and which environmental factors most shape diversity and composition under arid stress.

Her field work spans both peak summer and cool winter at the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve, where she sampled root-associated soil from three native species: *Haloxylon salicornicum* (Rimth), *Heliotropium kotschyi* (Kary), and *Dipterygium glaucum* (Safrawi). 

Initial soil data already capture the seasonal contrast at the heart of her hypothesis and the next phase, downstream sequencing analysis and integration of soil chemistry with microbial diversity, will test whether summer drought stress drives lower diversity and more clustered community structure than winter, and whether plant host identity leaves its own signature on the microbiome.

Her thesis is on track for defense in Fall 2026.
