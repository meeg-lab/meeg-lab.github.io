Hi! My name is Nesma and I'm a lover of nature. I have a passion for bioinformatics, biostatistics and their applications in microbial ecology. I am in my first year PhD at UAEU in the MEEG lab. My projects are rather diverse. The main focus of my thesis is the boreal forest microbiome in Norway, in which I aim to characterise fungal, bacterial and protist diversity across different soil compartments. This work is an example of one of our international collaborations. Secondly, I am interested in investigating environmental reservoirs of antimicrobial and metal resistance genes in arid soil. Most studies focus on sewage water, and especially hospital wastewater where we can find a large amount of AMR genes in the microbes but my focus is on the growing concern of microbial antimicrobial and metal co-resistance in our deserts here in the UAE. 

Following from my studies at Kingston University London, and my research over there with the department of Biochemistry, I aim to study how Generative AI technologies are changing the way we teach and study life sciences disciplines. Our ongoing study "Comparing the Effect of Generative AI (GenAI) Technologies on Life Sciences Higher Education in the UAE from Faculty and Student Perspectives" will be a national effort of gaining insights into how life science instruction is changing across various disciplines from Chemistry, Biology to Bioinformatics and Nutrition.

MEEG lab allows me to explore different interests of mine and develop the research skills necessary to be a successful microbial ecologist in the future.
