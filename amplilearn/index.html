What should my files look like?

AmpliLearn needs two separate files — sample IDs must match between them.

1 · OTU table (BIOM-style)

OTUs/ASVs in rows, samples in columns, raw whole-number counts. Optional last column named taxonomy.

#OTU IDSample_1Sample_2taxonomy
OTU00011520k__Bacteria; p__Firmicutes; …; g__Blautia
OTU0002841k__Bacteria; p__Bacteroidetes; …
2 · Sample metadata

One row per sample; first column = sample ID. Other columns are your variables.

sample_idgrouppH
Sample_1case6.2
Sample_2control7.1
Common mistakes
  • Percentages instead of raw counts — values like 0.0195. AmpliLearn rarefies and needs whole-number reads.
  • A hidden group row in the OTU table — group labels belong in the metadata file.
  • Taxonomy as the row name instead of a separate taxonomy column.
  • Mismatched sample IDs — watch spaces, capitalisation, and - vs _.
Step 2 of 5

Rarefaction

Samples sequenced to different depths look richer simply because more was sampled. Rarefaction subsamples every sample to a common depth so diversity can be compared fairly. Pick a depth where most curves plateau — samples below it are dropped.

Step 3 of 5

Alpha diversity

Alpha diversity is diversity within a sample. Observed counts OTUs present; Shannon and Simpson also weight by evenness; Pielou is evenness alone. Groups are compared with Mann-Whitney (two groups) or Kruskal-Wallis (more).

Step 4 of 5

Beta diversity & ordination

Beta diversity measures how different samples are from each other. We compute a distance matrix (Bray-Curtis uses abundances, Jaccard presence/absence, Aitchison is compositional) and reduce it to 2-D. PCoA preserves distances; NMDS preserves rank order — stress under 0.2 is usable.

Step 5 of 5

Taxonomic composition

A stacked bar plot of community composition — each bar is a sample, each colour a taxon, height is relative abundance. Choose a rank, how many top taxa to show, and optionally sort samples along an environmental gradient. Requires a taxonomy column in your OTU table.
