Step 1 of 6

DADA2 pipeline

This walkthrough mirrors the official DADA2 Pipeline Tutorial (1.16) by Benjamin Callahan. The starting point is demultiplexed, primer-free paired-end fastq files; the end product is an amplicon sequence variant (ASV) table — a higher-resolution analogue of the traditional OTU table. We simulate the mothur MiSeq SOP data: 2×250 Illumina MiSeq amplicon sequencing of the V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene from mouse gut samples collected longitudinally post-weaning.

The dataset
20 samples of mouse fecal 16S rRNA (V4 region), 2×250 paired-end MiSeq. All content and workflow adapted from the official DADA2 tutorial, maintained by Benjamin Callahan (CC-BY 4.0).
About DADA2 & citation

Tutorial source: DADA2 Pipeline Tutorial (1.16), maintained by Benjamin Callahan. Documentation License: CC-BY 4.0. This educational simulation reproduces the tutorial's steps, parameters, and example numbers.

Method citation: Callahan, B.J., McMurdie, P.J., Rosen, M.J., Han, A.W., Johnson, A.J.A., Holmes, S.P. (2016). DADA2: High-resolution sample inference from Illumina amplicon data. Nature Methods 13, 581–583. doi:10.1038/nmeth.3869

Standard filtering parameters used in the tutorial: truncLen=c(240,160), maxN=0, maxEE=c(2,2), truncQ=2, rm.phix=TRUE. The maxEE parameter (maximum expected errors) is a better filter than averaging quality scores.

For the full tutorial, taxonomy assignment (SILVA/RDP/GreenGenes), and phyloseq handoff, visit benjjneb.github.io/dada2/

Load your data

Two files. The OTU/ASV table is BIOM-style: OTUs/ASVs in rows, samples in columns, raw counts with an optional taxonomy column. The metadata has one row per sample with environmental variables. Sample IDs must match between both files. In the DADA2 tutorial, the ASV table is handed off to phyloseq for exactly this kind of analysis.

What should my files look like?

AmpliLearn needs two separate files — sample IDs must match between them.

1 · OTU table (BIOM-style)

OTUs/ASVs in rows, samples in columns, raw whole-number counts. Optional last column named taxonomy.

#OTU IDSample_1Sample_2taxonomy
OTU00011520k__Bacteria; p__Firmicutes; …; g__Blautia
OTU0002841k__Bacteria; p__Bacteroidetes; …
2 · Sample metadata

One row per sample; first column = sample ID. Other columns are your variables.

sample_idgrouppH
Sample_1case6.2
Sample_2control7.1
Common mistakes
  • Percentages instead of raw counts — values like 0.0195. AmpliLearn rarefies and needs whole-number reads.
  • A hidden group row in the OTU table — group labels belong in the metadata file.
  • Taxonomy as the row name instead of a separate taxonomy column.
  • Mismatched sample IDs — watch spaces, capitalisation, and - vs _.
Step 3 of 6

Rarefaction

Equalize sequencing depth by subsampling to a common read count, removing bias from differential sequencing effort. (This step is a common addition; the DADA2 tutorial itself proceeds directly to diversity analysis in phyloseq.)

Step 4 of 6

Alpha diversity

Within-sample diversity. The tutorial visualizes this with plot_richness(ps, measures=c("Shannon","Simpson")) — here we compute Shannon and Observed richness and compare groups statistically.

Step 5 of 6

Beta diversity

Between-sample dissimilarity and ordination. The tutorial ordinates Bray-Curtis distances with NMDS (ordinate(ps.prop, method="NMDS", distance="bray")), which cleanly separates early vs. late samples.

Step 6 of 6

Taxonomic composition

Community composition stacked bar plots — the tutorial's plot_bar(ps.top20, fill="Family"). In the mouse gut data, Bacteroidetes are well represented among the most abundant taxa.
